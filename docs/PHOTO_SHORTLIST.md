## Corpus coverage and method

- I inspected all 188 supplied still-image files across ten filename-labelled contact sheets.
- The byte-identical duplicate `20240610_181718_b108bb68-2e9e-4ead-9481-7da4568c5314 (1).jpg` was omitted after its identity with `20240610_181718_b108bb68-2e9e-4ead-9481-7da4568c5314.jpg` was confirmed. This left 187 byte-unique stills for visual review.
- All 78 HEICs and JPGs with problematic embedded profiles were rendered only as temporary Quick Look derivatives. The source files were not altered.
- The six MP4s were inventoried but not used for ranking because the stills were sufficient to identify the works and session bursts. A later human review may choose a frame from video only as a replacement for a weak still, never as an additional work.
- I grouped obvious bursts, in-progress photographs, alternative angles, and later healed/final views by visual identity before ranking.
- Every proposed work was judged twice: first as tattoo art, then as a photograph. A strong tattoo with a weak photograph is flagged rather than receiving a falsely high photographic score.
- I applied the documented 100-point rubric: tattoo craft/legibility **C /35**, photographic quality **P /25**, crop resilience **R /15**, portfolio contribution **V /15**, and brand fit **B /10**. Scores structure the comparison; they are not claims about artistic worth or legal clearance.
- Circular viability was assessed against a non-destructive desaturated thumbnail and a colour expanded view. `Strong` means the main design survives a centered circle; `workable` needs a deliberate focal point; `borderline` needs a crop test or replacement.

## Reject patterns and cautions

- Process-only images, stencils, practice skin, tattooing action shots, screenshots, and frames dominated by gloves/equipment were not treated as finished portfolio candidates.
- Severe fresh redness, blood/irritation, ointment glare, stencil residue, colour cast, blur, weak focus, and studio clutter lowered **photographic** scores even where the underlying tattoo appeared strong.
- Connected sleeves and older neighbouring tattoos make authorship boundaries ambiguous. Amy must identify which elements are hers before any crop or caption implies ownership of the whole limb.
- Visible faces occur in several selected or alternate photographs, especially the LOVE knuckles, shark, and skull work. Tighter crops reduce exposure but do not replace explicit publication permission.
- Chest, side, back, and upper-thigh placements deserve a more explicit privacy check even where a face is absent.
- No metadata has been stripped from originals. Before publication, create new derivatives, retain a private provenance record, and remove unnecessary location/device metadata only from those derivatives.
- Potential third-party characters, symbols, and fandom imagery need an IP/context review before commercial portfolio use; selection here is not legal clearance.
- Different skin tones are present but limited in the supplied corpus. Do not force representational balance by choosing a technically weak photograph; ask Amy for stronger healed images where possible.
