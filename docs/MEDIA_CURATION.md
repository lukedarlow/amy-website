## Non-destructive workflow

1. Inventory filenames, format, pixel dimensions, orientation, file size, and capture date where present.
2. Detect exact and perceptual duplicates.
3. Group photographs by the same tattoo/session.
4. Generate filename-labelled contact sheets from temporary derivatives. Preserve originals.
5. Remove technical rejects and permission risks from consideration; never delete them.
6. Score the strongest frame from each tattoo and create a diverse shortlist of about 40 works.
7. Review circular-crop viability and the uncropped lightbox version.
8. Build a proposed set of 25 with a written rationale and category coverage.
9. Ask Amy/Luke to confirm, replace, or veto selections before any production processing.
10. Record the approved filenames and any crop notes in a future manifest.

## Scoring rubric

Score each candidate out of 100:

- **Tattoo craft and legibility - 35:** clean execution, readable design, confident line/colour/value, and a finished result that represents Amy fairly.
- **Photographic quality - 25:** focus, exposure, colour, glare control, useful resolution, and minimal motion/noise.
- **Composition and crop resilience - 15:** tattoo is clearly framed; both a circle thumbnail and larger original-ratio view work without cutting off essential art.
- **Portfolio contribution - 15:** adds needed subject, technique, colour, scale, placement, or visual rhythm rather than duplicating another selection.
- **Brand fit - 10:** supports custom storytelling, birds/botanicals, whimsy, colour, illustration, or multidisciplinary precision.

Use scores to structure judgment, not replace it.

## Reject or flag criteria

- Unrecoverable blur, missed focus, severe compression, or insufficient resolution.
- Harsh glare, extreme colour cast, deep shadow, or overexposure that hides the work.
- Fresh redness, blood, swelling, stencil residue, or ointment glare that distracts from the tattoo.
- Awkward perspective or anatomy distortion.
- Busy backgrounds, unrelated existing tattoos, clothing, or studio clutter dominating the frame.
- A crop that cannot form a useful circle without losing essential content.
- A weaker duplicate or near-duplicate of the same tattoo.
- Work not made by Amy, unclear authorship, or unclear client permission.
- Visible faces or identifying information without explicit publication approval.

Flag borderline images for discussion rather than silently excluding meaningful work.

## Portfolio balance

The final 25 should show range without becoming a checklist. During selection, inspect coverage across:

- birds and botanicals;
- fine line and geometric work;
- realism and illustrative work;
- colour and black/grey;
- small and larger pieces;
- different body placements and compositions;
- different skin tones, where the source corpus and permissions allow;
- strong custom-story pieces that communicate Amy's differentiator.

Avoid letting one prolific session, subject, client, or photographic setup dominate.

## Gallery-specific review

Each chosen image needs two judgments:

- **Circle thumbnail:** recognizable at small size, centered intentionally, no essential element clipped, acceptable when desaturated, and distinct from neighbours.
- **Expanded image:** colour is believable, detail withstands enlargement, orientation is correct, and surrounding context does not distract.

The website should create circle masks non-destructively. Do not export permanently circular originals.

## Rights, privacy, and metadata

Before publication, confirm client permission and Amy's authorship. Decide whether EXIF metadata should be stripped from public derivatives to avoid leaking location/device information while retaining an internal provenance record. Never strip metadata from the originals.
